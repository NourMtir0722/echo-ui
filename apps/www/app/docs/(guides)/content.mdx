# Introduction

**Tactile components you can feel. The design system that keeps your AI on-brand.** Echo UI is a
tactile design system for React. It's one design system, not a bundle of components: every part is
made from the same material and follows the same rules, from molded caps in recessed
wells to emissive LCD readouts and real press travel. One colour themes all of them, and they
press, sound and move the same way. Motion and sound are token families of their own, next to
colour and type, so a button does not just look pressed, it moves and it clicks. And none of that is paid for in access: every component is keyboard-complete,
named for a screen reader, works in forced colors and under reduced motion, and holds WCAG 2.2 AA
on every seed you give it.

Your style goes on top. Your mood boards become styles, each product wears the one that fits it
(its colours, type, corners and finish), and your AI agent builds in it. The feel stays Echo's.

## The workflow

1. **Your mood boards become styles.** Echo's Design Eye reads them into your own private library.
2. **A new app picks its style.** `init` sets the project up, and Style Fit picks the style that fits.
3. **Your agent builds the screen.** It asks Echo which component does each job, then copies it in,
   in your style. One setting later, like "rounder", is one line: `style set`.
4. **When it slips, Echo hands it back.** A check runs on every file your agent saves, and when it
   says it's done, Echo's tests (design and axe) run on the pages it changed until they pass.
5. **Next product, next style.** The same components and the same feel, in another look.

[The workflow](/docs/workflow) follows one builder through all five, with every command.

## How it works

Echo is a **registry, not an npm package.** `npx echo-ui-cli` copies each component's source into
your project, and from then on it is yours: read it, change it, delete what you do not need. There
is no Echo runtime to upgrade and no black box to work around — only `echo-ui-core`, the grammar
the components import for theme, sound and motion.

The registry has <ItemCount />, and every one of them takes its colours from a single seed.

## What makes it different

- **One seed, every colour.** You choose an accent, a chassis and a paper colour. Every surface,
  cap, well, glow and text colour is derived from them in OKLCH, and every text colour is checked
  against WCAG AA on each background it appears on. See [Theming](/docs/theming).
- **Your look on top.** A style from your own mood boards sets the colours, type, radius, finish
  and density, on a dark device or a light one, and its rules go in `DESIGN.md` for your agent.
  See [Styles](/docs/styles).
- **Sound without audio files.** Sounds are short synth recipes played through WebAudio. They respect
  mute, a blurred tab and reduced motion. See [Motion & sound](/docs/motion-and-sound).
- **Behaviour from Base UI.** Dialogs, menus, tabs and selects wrap [Base UI](https://base-ui.com), so
  focus, keyboard and ARIA are handled by a maintained library; Echo adds the material and the feel.
- **Accessible before it is pretty.** WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, not the goal: a control without a
  name does not compile, and every component is tested for focus, target size, forced colours and
  reflow. Every component page ends with what was tested and what is still open.
- **An agent that stays on your style.** `init` tells Claude Code, Codex or Cursor it's in an Echo
  project, gives it a way to ask which component does each job, and runs a check on every file it
  saves. Tools without a terminal get the same docs as `llms.txt`, `catalog.json` and an
  [MCP server](/docs/mcp). See [For AI agents](/docs/ai) and [The workflow](/docs/workflow).

## Tools

- [Theme generator](/generator) — one colour in, the whole device relights.
- [Style switcher](/styles) — switch between the styles Echo publishes, on real components.
- [Sound audition](/audition) — hear every sound token, and tune a recipe's pitch, length, volume and
  bend.
- [Tactile bench](/tactile) — one button wired with `useTactile`: press it with a pointer, then with
  the keyboard, and it feels the same.
