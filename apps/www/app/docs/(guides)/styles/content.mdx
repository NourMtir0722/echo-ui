# Styles

A style is a look your product wears on top of Echo. It changes the vocabulary: the colours, the
type, how round and how dense the device is, what its plastic feels like, and the rules an agent
builds screens by. It never changes the grammar: one key light, what each material is for, state in
words and shape, motion as physics, sound as feedback, and the accessibility floor. Two products in
two styles look like two devices from the same maker, not two skins on one.

## Dark or light

The chassis decides. A dark chassis makes a device with lit screens; a pale one makes a light
device, like a white calculator, with screens of paper glass printed in ink. There's no mode to
switch: a style carries its chassis, so it carries its device. [Theming](/docs/theming) says how
each is derived.

## Where styles come from

Styles live in your own library, `~/Documents/styles` (or wherever `ECHO_STYLES_DIR` points). It's
yours and private: a style is your taste, reused across products, and nothing in the library goes
into a project unless you wear it. Echo publishes a library of its own: Echo's own style, `echo`,
and **Drafting Index**, `drafting-index`, a light device drawn in one black ink, hollow until it's
on. You can wear either without a board, and the [Styles page](/styles) lets you switch between
them on real components first.

Each of your styles is a folder:

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `<name>.md` | The foundation: what the style is made of, the rules underneath it, where it fits, and how Echo wears it. |
| `style.json` | What Echo applies: its settings, its rules and decisions for the agent, what it refused, and what you kept of what the slop police bans. |
| `assets/` | The evidence: each reference, its sampled colours, and magnified details. |

## From a moodboard

`init` writes Echo's Design Eye into your project, a skill your agent uses. Save your moodboard as a
PNG or JPEG, and ask your agent to read it. It asks what the product is for, if the project doesn't
already say; groups the board by visual logic; samples each reference's real colours with
`style sample`; reads its type, its buttons, and its surfaces and colour as three systems; asks you,
in one message, what the board can't settle, each with the answer it suggests (the colour, counted
in tiles; a tile that doesn't fit; which part is the main action); writes one foundation and one
`style.json` per style it finds into your library, with the decisions every
screen keeps (how something is made important, what the main action looks like, how the product
talks); and checks each with `style check`. A tell on the board itself (a sparkle for "AI", a strip of
round-number stats) is left out of the style, and named. A board drawn in line, with outlined parts
that still look pressable, gets `render: drawn`: Echo's parts outlined in the ink instead of molded
and lit.

A foundation you wrote before Echo, with no `style.json`, doesn't need its board read again: Design
Eye writes its Echo part from the document.

## Choosing one

Echo's Style Fit, the next skill `init` writes, picks the style a product wears. Ask your agent which
style fits. It works out who the product is for, places it on the seven axes styles are placed on
(warmth, volume, density, formality, era, craft, ornament) with how far a style may drift on each,
and has `style fit` measure every style in your library and Echo's against that. It reads the best of
them, argues for one in a decision it saves to your library, and wears it.

## Wearing one

```sh
npx echo-ui-cli style use <name>
```

`style use` writes the style's settings to `echo.json` and `echo-theme.css`, says what the theme
corrected for contrast and which fonts to get, and puts the style's rules in `DESIGN.md`, so every
agent working on the project keeps to them. What Style Fit adapted for this product goes in
`DESIGN.md` too, beside what the product is for. The foundation and the board stay in your library.
When you know the style from the start, `init --style <name>` sets the project up already wearing it.

## The slop police

Some combinations read as made by AI the moment they appear: a cream ground with a terracotta
accent; near-black with one acid green; indigo on white in a stock sans; cream, brass and a serif for
anything "premium"; one stock face for everything. Each ingredient is fine on its own. Together,
they're what a model reaches for when nobody decided.

`style check` stops a style whose own settings make one of these pairings, names the ingredients, and
asks the question for you. `style use` won't wear it, and `style fit` marks it on the list. You can
keep one on purpose: your reason goes in the style's `keeps`, and it's never asked again. Your agent
can't keep one for you; it asks. In the code, `check` looks for the same kind of thing around Echo's
parts: gradient text, an emoji as an icon, the sparkle for AI, "Get started", a stand-in name or
photo, a stripe down one edge of a card, spaced capitals made by hand. What your style kept isn't
flagged; a stand-in name or photo always is.

## Changing one setting

```sh
npx echo-ui-cli style set radius 0.8 finish 0.7
```

`style set` moves one setting, or a few, in this project only: rounder, glossier, denser, another
accent or typeface. It checks every value before it writes anything, writes the theme and
`DESIGN.md` again, and prints the line that puts it back. The style stays on, marked with what
moved, and `style use` puts it back whole. Your library isn't touched. `style set` alone lists
where each setting is, and `default` gives one back to Echo. A whole new look is a new style, not a
pile of settings.

## Where a look lives

A project's look is in three files, and one of them is the source:

| File | What it holds | Written by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `echo.json` | **The source.** The seed (colours, shape, type), and the style it wears: its rules, decisions, fonts and what it refused, with what `style set` has moved since. | `init --seed`, `init --style`, `style use`, `style set` |
| `echo-theme.css` | The theme, rendered from `echo.json`'s seed as plain CSS. | The same commands, every time the seed moves |
| `DESIGN.md` | The look in Echo's terms for an agent, the style's rules beside it, stamped with the CLI version and the seed it was written from. | The same commands; `add` and `update` keep it current |

So change the look with a command, never by editing the theme by hand: `style set` for one
setting, `style use` for a whole style. Each writes all three, from the one seed. `check` says when
`DESIGN.md` was written by another version or from another seed.

A teammate who clones the project gets all of it: the seed, the style's rules and `DESIGN.md` are
in the repository, so their agent builds in the same look. What stays on your machine is the
style's foundation and its board, in your library: needed to read the style again or wear it in
another product, not to build this one.

Two places hand out the same look without a terminal. The [theme generator](/generator)
downloads a `DESIGN.md` for a seed, and the MCP server's `wear_style` gives a style's theme CSS and
`DESIGN.md` for a project with no terminal. Both open by telling an agent that does have one to run
`init` instead, so the project ends up with all three files.

## What a style can't do

- **Move the grammar.** Parts that can't be pressed, like flat cards standing in for buttons,
  aren't Echo: a board drawn in line still gets keys that travel, as a drawn device
  ([`render: drawn`](/docs/theming#molded-or-drawn)), and one that wants glass or flat paint gets
  the closest molded finish. A style that wants a second accent for errors keeps state in words and
  shape. The foundation says what it refused and what it got instead.
- **Keep its exact accent, on a molded device.** Every accent there is held to one lightness, so a
  lit cap reads the same in every style: an acid yellow comes out olive. `style check` says when it
  moves. A drawn device keeps the accent as it is, as the flat fill for what's on.
- **Go past Echo's type scale.** It stops at the display step, 40px. Display type the size of the
  page isn't in it; a loud style is loud through weight and its accent.
- **Put a dark screen on a light body**, or a pale one on a dark body: one text colour can't be
  readable on both.

## The commands

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `style list` | Echo's styles and yours, with the device each makes. |
| `style show <name>` | One style in full, and where its foundation is. |
| `style fit --profile "W4 V2 D1 F2 E3 C3 O3"` | Every style, measured against a product's place on the seven axes. `--tolerance` sets how far each may drift; 0 locks an axis. |
| `style use <name>` | Wears a style. |
| `style set <setting> <value> …` | Moves one setting of this project's seed, or several; alone, it lists them. |
| `style check <folder>` | Whether a `style.json` is one Echo can apply, which device it makes, what contrast corrects, what the slop police stops, and what the style leaves undecided. |
| `style sample <image> --out <folder>` | A reference's real colours and magnified details: Design Eye's evidence. |
