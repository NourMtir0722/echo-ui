# Privacy

*Last updated October 31, 2026*

Echo UI is made by Nawwara Studio LLC, a company registered in Wyoming, USA. This page says what
Echo's site, its command-line tool and its hosted MCP server collect, which is very little, and
why. Questions go to <Email />.

## The site

- We count visits with Umami, on a server we run ourselves. It sets no cookies and doesn't follow
  you across other sites. We see totals: pages viewed, the site you came from, and your country,
  browser and device type. If your browser sends Do Not Track, you aren't counted.
- No advertising.
- Your browser keeps three things on your own device: the colours you chose for the site, whether
  you turned its sound on, and, until you close the tab, where the docs sidebar was scrolled. None
  of them leaves your device. Clearing your browser's site data removes them.
- The site's fonts, scripts and images are served from <Host /> itself, apart from the script that
  counts visits, which comes from our own server, stats.nawwara.studio. It loads nothing from anyone
  else.
- The pages that show Echo's supporters (the island, and the Echos at the foot of some pages) ask
  Echo's own supporters server who's on the island. The request carries nothing about you, and that
  server's logs hold a request's path, status and timing, never more.

## The hosted MCP server

This is the server ChatGPT, Claude and other AI tools connect to when you use Echo there, through
Echo's plugin or as a connector, at <McpHost />.

- It receives what a tool is called with: a search, a component's name, a style's settings, or
  code to check. It uses that to answer, and keeps none of it. Code sent to the check is checked
  and forgotten.
- It needs no account, and knows nothing about who you are.
- What you say in ChatGPT or Claude stays with them, under their own privacy policies. The server
  only sees the tool calls.

## Hosting, and request logs

Cloudflare hosts the site and the server. Like any web host, it handles each request, and it can
keep a record of it for up to 7 days: what was asked for, when, the response, and the address the
request came from. Echo uses those records only to keep the site and the server running and to
stop abuse. The server also counts the requests from each address over ten seconds, and the size of
the large ones over a minute, so a runaway script is refused for a few seconds, or a minute.

## The CLI, echo-ui-cli

It reads Echo's registry from <Host /> and installs packages from npm, under npm's own privacy
policy. It sends nothing about you or your project to Echo, and has no telemetry. Its page tests
run in your own browser, on your own machine.

## Your styles

Styles you make with Echo's Design Eye stay where you keep them: on your computer, or in your chat.
They aren't stored on Echo's servers.

## Tips

When you send a tip, you pay on the payment provider's own page, under its privacy policy. Echo
receives what the provider passes on to a seller, which can include your name, your email address
and the amount, and never your card details. We keep these as business records for as long as tax
law requires.

Right after you pay, the payment page brings you back to this site, where you can give a name and up
to two links for Echo's island (the supporters' page, /supporters). Echo's server checks with the
payment provider that the payment is complete, and keeps what you sent, so that you can change it.
The payment provider also tells the server about each payment, renewal, refund and dispute, and the
server keeps when you paid and whether it was monthly or once: that's how long your Echo stays awake
on the island, and how its card counts the times you've supported. It never sees your card, and
keeps neither your email address nor the amount. To know that two payments are the same person's,
it keeps a fingerprint of your address, made with a secret of its own, that can't be turned back
into the address. Right after you pay, it also emails you a link to that page, at the address you
gave the payment provider: it sends that one email through Cloudflare, which delivers it, and
doesn't keep the address. What you send is shown publicly on the island as soon as you send it, as
you sent it, with an Echo whose colour Echo chooses, and your links are marked as paid for. Ask,
and they come down: your name, your links, your payments and the fingerprint are erased, and only
your Echo's number stays taken, so no one else is given it.

## When you write to us

We keep your message and your address to answer you, and delete them when you ask, unless the law
requires us to keep them.

## Your choices
