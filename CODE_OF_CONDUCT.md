# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying. Argue with the work, not with the person who did it.

## Our pledge

Everyone who takes part in this project — issues, pull requests, discussions — is entitled to a
harassment-free experience regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education,
socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We are building a library whose stated floor is WCAG 2.2 AA because we think the people using
software should not have to be lucky. The same expectation applies to the people building it.

## What this looks like in practice

Things that make this project better:

- Reading the thing before responding to it. Echo's documents are long because the reasoning
  matters; a review that engages with the reasoning is worth ten that engage with the diff.
- Saying "I was wrong" quickly and without ceremony.
- Being specific. "This breaks keyboard focus on Safari" is a contribution; "this feels off" is a
  start, and worth saying, but finish it if you can.
- Asking. Nobody here was born knowing how a registry's cache headers work.

Things that are not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing someone's private information — a physical or email address, anything else — without
  their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening a settled decision over and over, or turning a review thread
  into a war of attrition.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — the repository, its issues and pull requests, its
discussions — and whenever someone is representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report conduct issues privately to **support@echo-ui.com**, with "conduct" in the subject. Reports
are read by the maintainer and are not shared further without the reporter's agreement, except where something has to be
disclosed to act on it.

Every report is looked into. The response is proportionate: most things need a private word, some
need a public correction, a few need someone to be removed from the project's spaces. Whoever
reported it is told what happened.

Maintainers who do not hold themselves to this document can expect to be held to it by everyone
else, and should be.
